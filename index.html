💡 In Simple Terms: How Computers Pick Random Characters

Computers generate randomness as numbers from 0 to 255 (256 possible values in 1 byte). To pick a character for your password, the program has to split those 256 numbers among all the letters in your character pool.

Character Pool: A B C D E F G
Pool size 7
Random byte range 0 – 255
256 ÷ 7 = 36 remainder 4
⚠ 4 characters receive an extra byte mapping — bias is unavoidable with modulo.
Step 2

Two Methods of Character Selection

Both methods use secure random numbers. The difference is how those numbers are assigned to characters.

Method A

Modulo Mapping

randomByte % poolSize

Takes the remainder after division. Fast, but creates unfair probabilities.

🎟️ Simple Analogy:

Imagine handing out 256 raffle tickets to 7 people. 4 people get 37 tickets, while 3 people only get 36 tickets. The first 4 people have a higher chance of winning!

⚠ Unequal probability
Method B

Rejection Sampling

Discards leftover numbers outside the even range and draws a new random number.

🎟️ Simple Analogy:

Throw away the 4 leftover raffle tickets so everyone gets exactly 36 tickets. Now every single person has an identical 100% fair chance!

✓ Equal probability

📖

Understanding Your Results in Simple Terms

📊 Chi-Squared (χ²) Statistic

Chi-squared (χ²) measures how far the observed distribution differs from the expected distribution. Larger values indicate a larger deviation, although the sample size must also be considered when interpreting the result.

🎯 Expected %

The exact theoretical target percentage ($100\% \div \text{pool size}$) each character would receive in a perfectly uniform distribution.

📈 Max Deviation

The single largest difference between a character's actual percentage and its expected fair target percentage.

Can you tell which one has statistical bias just by reading them?

Answer: You cannot tell by looking at individual passwords!

A password can look completely random even when the underlying process gives certain letters a 0.4% higher chance of selection. Human eyes cannot spot mathematical bias in a short string.

Only statistical testing across tens of thousands of samples reveals the flaw. That is why proper cryptographic implementation matters so much!

Security relies on math, not appearance.

Why This Matters
