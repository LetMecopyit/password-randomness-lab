Can You Spot Biased Randomness?

An interactive experiment showing why random-looking passwords are not always generated equally.

Two character generators can both produce strings that look random. But one may give certain characters a slightly higher probability than others.

Step 1

Choose a Character Pool

Modulo bias becomes easier to demonstrate with pools whose size does not divide evenly into 256, the range of a single random byte.

Pool: A B C D E F G
Pool size 7
Random byte range 0 – 255
256 ÷ 7 = 36 remainder 4
⚠ 4 characters receive an extra byte mapping — bias is unavoidable with modulo.
Step 2

Two Methods of Character Selection

Both methods use cryptographically secure random bytes. The difference is how those bytes are mapped to the character pool.

Method A

Modulo Mapping

randomByte % poolSize

Simple. Common. Potentially biased.

⚠ Unequal probability
Method B

Rejection Sampling

Values outside the largest evenly divisible range are discarded. A new random value is drawn.

✓ Equal probability

Can you tell which one has statistical bias?

You cannot determine randomness quality by looking at a few generated passwords.

A password can look completely random even when the underlying character-selection process is slightly biased. Individual outputs from both methods are visually indistinguishable.

Only statistical analysis over large samples reveals the difference. That is exactly what the experiment above demonstrates.

Implementation matters.

Why This Matters
